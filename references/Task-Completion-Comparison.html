A small moment of completion.

Inspired by your day-and-night reference: a drawn tick, a moving strike-through and a brief burst from the checkbox. Try each effect on a task, watch the celebration, a short pause, then the task slides away and the rows below move up.

Suggested combination

Everyday: confetti, or the quiet ripple if you want less decoration. Dark mode: moons and stars. 31 October: bats in either colour scheme, taking precedence over the night effect. These are proposed selection rules, not active seasonal behaviour in this demo.

Keep the tick and strike-through common to every version. Let the celebration finish (800–1100ms), pause for 100ms, slide the task left out of view over 450ms, then close its space over 300ms so the tasks below move smoothly upward. When the .us-task-completed-toggle button has aria-pressed="true" (Show completed is on), the celebration still plays but slide/collapse is skipped. When it is off or absent, the normal exit sequence runs. Use the Show completed button in each sample to compare; turning it on expands completed rows into view over 300ms. Turn off “Slide completed row away” to inspect the completed state. Slow motion doubles the whole sequence. Undo restores the checkbox without another celebration. With reduced motion, update the state immediately and skip particles and row movement.

The reference uses a fixed-size, automatic looping stage. This comparison uses responsive task rows and user-triggered effects. It is a local appearance prototype: no API calls, saved task changes or production theme edits. When integrating, effects must follow the real completion outcome and respect the existing search, completed-filter, focus and partial-update lifecycle.
