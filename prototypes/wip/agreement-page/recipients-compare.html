1. Sentence and list (as built)

The template as it stands: one information alert with a sentence naming the first three, a count of who will not receive it, and "Show all" opening a grid of every contact.

  • Reads well at a glance; one line until opened.
  • Problems are a clause in the sentence, easy to read past.

2. Recipient chips

The contacts as chips after "To", like an email client. The first five show; a "+3 more" chip opens the rest. A contact who will not receive it is a red chip with the reason on hover.

  • Familiar from email; every name visible without opening a list.
  • No role or group; the reason for a red chip is only on hover.

3. Avatars and groups

An avatar stack (as on the Contacts panel), the count and a badge per group, so the send reads as "SBU 3". The list opens as rows: avatar, name, role · group · email, and a status badge.

  • Matches the Contacts panel the send came from; groups make the audience obvious.
  • Taller when opened; avatars add little when the names are listed anyway.

4. Counts and table

One line: the count, the groups, and how many will not receive it. The list opens as a compact table (Name, Role, Group, Email, Status), like the native Recipients report, capped at 260px with its own scroll and the header kept in view.

  • Densest; scans like a report and handles a long list.
  • Plainer; no names until opened.

5. Problems first

A short information line with the count and groups. Anyone who will not receive the email gets a separate warning naming them and why, so it is fixed (or accepted) before sending. With no problems there is only the information line.

  • Puts the thing staff must act on in front; quiet when all is well.
  • Two alerts when there are problems; the full list is still one click away.
