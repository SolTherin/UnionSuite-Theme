1. Contact page v3: Change history grid

The contact page's Admin tab audit log: a native Query Menu, one row per field change, styled by the theme. Nothing to build but the IQA, and it sorts, pages and exports like every report. Every change stands alone: a save that changed three fields is three unrelated rows, the record changed and the field share one column, and long values (a term's comment) stretch the row. No filter by area without the IQA's own filter fields.

2. v1 mock-up: Change History timeline (us-changelog us-changelog--timeline)

The agreement mock-up's History tab on theme tokens: each save is one entry on a rail, with the person's initials, their name, a coloured badge for the kind of change (Created, Added, Updated, Status change, Removed) and the full time, a sentence saying what changed, then each field as its old and new value side by side. The v1 search and type filter become the theme's search and type chips. Saves read as one event, but every entry repeats the full date, and the 25 saves take about 2,550px.

3. Activity feed record cards (US-ACTIVITY-FEED, a "change" type)

The record card used for communications, one card per field change: the area as the card's category, who as its sender, a headline for the record and the change itself as the preview line; the field, old and new value again in its details. Months, the date range, search across loaded changes and Show more come from the feed. One design for every history on the page, but a card is about 78px for one field, a three-field save is three cards, and a chevron that opens to what the closed card already says.

4. Ledger by day and save (us-changelog)

One heading per day; under it each save is one line in plain words ("Mary O'Conner updated the term Staff entitled to a half day off on Fridays") with the area's icon and the time, and its fields under the sentence: the field, the old value struck through, the new one. The record names the save; the fields line up. Area chips (Terms, Increases, Tasks…) and the search sit in the funnel, with the count beside the title, as the terms panel. The 25 saves take about 2,250px (2,550px on the timeline); each further field adds a 20px line, and a day with several saves reads as a short paragraph. Same template and IQA as option 2.

5. Ledger on a rail (us-changelog us-changelog--rail)

Option 4 without the day headings. Each save keeps its sentence, area icon and field lines, and its date and time stack on the right as on the record cards in option 3 (1 Oct over 4:38 pm), beside the sentence and first field line rather than above them. A line joins the icons of one day's saves, the cards' rail, and stops at the day's last save; the next day starts a new line after a gap and a thin rule. The dates line up down the right edge, and with no day headings and no rule between one day's saves the 25 saves take about 1,770px, the shortest here. Same template and IQA as options 2 and 4.

6. Change first — a reading timeline

The record leads, with its first value change immediately below it; the person and time sit quietly underneath. A continuous icon rail connects the saves across days, with a date label only when the day changes. Further fields slide open inside the same entry. Try the half-day term or “Agreement details”. Best for catching up on what changed, with less repeated wording; comparing every field takes an extra click.

7. Browse & inspect — a focused comparison

A compact, connected feed on the left is the index; select a save to read every field side by side on the right. The selection stays visible while you browse, and long values have room to wrap. At narrow widths, the comparison slides open beneath the selected entry. Best for investigating a particular edit; less useful for reading every change in one pass. The half-day term is selected initially to demonstrate a save with two fields.
