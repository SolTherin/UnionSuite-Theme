The "do not click" button

The highest-risk idea in the register, and the only one that plants a control that isn't really there. Everything else decorates a state the product already produces; this one adds a thing, and to a staff member an unexplained button in a CRM is indistinguishable from a bug until they click it.

So the mockup is really about placement. Click the button. Then look at the second panel, which is the same button somewhere it must never go.

Risk: highest in the register Conflicts with phishing training Once per user, ever

Safe placement — the About screen Non-transactional. Nothing here can change a member, a payment or a status.

Do not click

Never A joke button sitting beside "Delete record" on a live member. Even if it does nothing, the user cannot know that before clicking, and the thing next to it is irreversible.

The conflict worth raising before anyone builds this

Organisations run phishing simulations and tell staff, in as many words, do not click unexpected things. This egg rewards someone for clicking an unexpected button, in the same building, on the same week. The person who reports it to IT as suspicious is the person who did exactly what they were trained to do — and they will feel silly, which is the opposite of the intended effect.

Three things make that manageable, and they are all design decisions rather than code:

  1. The payoff carries the reassurance. "Nothing happened. Nothing was going to." is the second line for a reason — it answers the only question the user actually has.
  2. It confesses that it is deliberate. The moment it resolves, the user knows a person put it there. An egg that stays mysterious here is an egg that gets logged as an incident.
  3. The service desk is told it exists. The first ticket will be "there is a strange button on the About page", and somebody should already have the answer rather than opening an investigation.
Recommendation Ship it on the About screen, once per user, exactly as mocked. That keeps the delight and removes every version of "did I just break something?"
