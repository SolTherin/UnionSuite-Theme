Try a normal search too — nguyen, smith, or anything else. The point of the mockup is that ordinary searching is completely untouched.

Rules the mockup encodes

  1. Nothing is ever suppressed. In B-ii and B-iii the results render exactly as normal and the line is additive. Only B-i changes anything, and only in a state that is empty anyway.
  2. Whole-string match, case and whitespace insensitive. help is an egg; help desk officer is a real search and behaves like one. Try both.
  3. * never runs. A literal wildcard over the member table is genuinely expensive, and a joke printed over a thirty-second spinner is not a joke. It is intercepted before the query, and offers the paged "browse all members" path instead.
  4. The count threshold is the design question, not the copy. "Good luck" at 87 results is funny; at 9 it is confusing. Here it fires above 50 on a plain two-word personal name. Search john smith versus mary o'brien to see the threshold work.
  5. me is quietly useful as well as funny — the line carries a link to your own staff record. That is the version people will actually use, which is what turns an egg into a feature.
  6. overtime is not in the dictionary. A union membership system winking about unpaid hours is the wrong joke in this building. Cut at the register stage, recorded here so it is not re-suggested later.
